# FunnelIQ — Analytical Report

Consolidated findings from all six PRD work packages. Each section is a
condensed summary; full detail (complete tables, methodology, caveats) is
in the linked `docs/*.md` file, generated by the corresponding
`analysis/*.py` script — re-run any of them against a refreshed
`funnel_marketing_data.csv` to regenerate these numbers.

All 3,500 raw rows were cleaned deterministically before any analysis
(10 exact duplicates dropped, 2 non-purchaser profit nulls filled with 0.0,
31 rows with unrecoverable missing targets dropped) — **3,459 clean rows**
underlie every finding below. See `analysis/data_cleaning.py`.

## Executive summary

The single most important, recurring pattern across this analysis: **campaigns
budgeted 2,000-5,000 meaningfully outperform both smaller and larger ones** -
not just in profit, but in conversion rate. It shows up independently in
Work Package 1 (conversion peaks at the Medium budget tier), and again in
Work Package 6 (profit is 4-10x higher for that budget range than any
other). This is the highest-leverage, most actionable finding in the
dataset: budget allocation matters more than almost any other lever
examined here.

The second consistent theme: `referred` and `ltv_months` are the strongest
behavioral signals across multiple models (upsell classification, LTV
regression, super-customer scoring) - customers who refer others and
customers with high lifetime value are systematically different from the
rest of the base, and worth identifying early.

## Work Package 1 — EDA & Data Cleaning

Full detail: [`docs/eda_findings.md`](docs/eda_findings.md)

- Strongest correlate of `cumulative_profit`: `ltv_months` (+0.845), then
  `upsell` (+0.651), `referred` (+0.583).
- Diminishing returns on ad spend: leads generated per $1,000 fall from
  19.7 (Low tier) to 12.9 (Medium) to 8.3 (High).
- Conversion rate is **not** monotonic with budget - it peaks at the Medium
  tier (8.2%) and *falls* at the High tier (5.4%), despite High-tier
  campaigns generating the most raw leads.

## Work Package 2 — LTV Regression

Full detail: [`docs/ltv_regression_findings.md`](docs/ltv_regression_findings.md)

Target: `ltv_months`. `cumulative_profit` excluded from every feature set
(data leakage guard - it's only known after a customer's lifecycle ends).

| Model | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|
| XGBoost | 2.909 ± 0.194 | 0.945 ± 0.008 |
| **LightGBM** | **2.869 ± 0.175** | **0.946 ± 0.007** |
| CatBoost | 2.873 ± 0.180 | 0.946 ± 0.007 |

All three models agree closely and predict `ltv_months` well. `closed` and
`calls_to_closed` dominate feature importance across all of them -
conversion-effort signals predict retention length more than
acquisition-side features like `ad_budget`.

## Work Package 3 — Upsell Classification

Full detail: [`docs/upsell_classification_findings.md`](docs/upsell_classification_findings.md)

Target: `upsell` (58%/42% class split - moderate imbalance, handled via
`scale_pos_weight`/`auto_class_weights`).

| Model | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Majority-class baseline | 0.580 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.500 |
| Business heuristic (LTV high & CAC low) | 0.642 | 0.681 | 0.279 | 0.395 | 0.592 |
| XGBoost | 0.813 | 0.774 | 0.783 | 0.778 | 0.884 |
| LightGBM | 0.811 | 0.767 | 0.788 | 0.777 | 0.883 |
| **CatBoost** | **0.815** | 0.772 | **0.793** | **0.782** | **0.887** |

The ML models roughly **double the F1 score** of the best simple
business-rule heuristic (0.782 vs 0.395) - a rule like "high LTV, low CAC"
misses most real upsell opportunities (recall 0.279). `referred` and
`ltv_months` are the top predictive features across all three models.

## Work Package 4 — Super-Customer Score

Full detail: [`docs/super_customer_score_findings.md`](docs/super_customer_score_findings.md)

Target: `referred`. CatBoost only, using its native categorical handling
for a new `budget_tier` feature (Low/Medium/High - no manual encoding).
Grid search over learning_rate x depth x iterations (12 combinations, 5-fold
CV each).

**Best configuration**: learning_rate=0.03, depth=4, iterations=200 -
F1=0.768, ROC-AUC=0.873. Notably, the *shallowest, fastest* configuration
won; more depth/iterations did not improve results, a sign of overfitting
at higher complexity on this data size.

The trained model is committed (`models/super_customer_score.cbm`, ~73KB)
and served live via `POST /api/score-lead` (JWT-protected), returning a
0-100 likelihood score for a lead becoming a high-retention, high-upsell,
high-referral "Super-Customer".

**Caveat**: this dataset's rows are campaign/cohort aggregates, not
individual-lead records, so some training features (e.g. `closed`,
`purchased`) describe outcomes a genuinely brand-new lead wouldn't have
yet. The scoring pipeline matches the PRD's literal spec; a production
intake form for a truly new lead should only collect fields actually known
at that time.

## Work Package 5 — Follow-Up Funnel Analysis

Full detail: [`docs/followup_funnel_findings.md`](docs/followup_funnel_findings.md)

| Stage | Total | Drop-off from prior stage |
|---|---|---|
| `followup_1` | 75,967 | - |
| `followup_2` | 56,467 | 25.7% |
| `followup_3` | 45,962 | 18.6% |
| `followup_4` | 41,194 | 10.4% |
| `followup_5` | 29,157 | **29.2%** |

**Anomalous stage**: `followup_4` -> `followup_5`. Every earlier transition
shrinks (attrition naturally falls as the remaining cohort gets more
engaged) - this is the one transition that breaks that pattern, jumping
back up to the highest drop-off of the whole funnel.

**Average follow-up cycles for closed deals**: exactly **5.0**, always -
verified directly (`followup_5 == not_closed + closed` holds for 100% of
rows). A deal cannot close before completing all five follow-ups, which
makes the stage-4-to-5 anomaly especially costly: it's disqualifying the
most-engaged remaining leads right before the point that determines whether
they can close at all.

**Recommendation**: pilot a change specifically at that touchpoint (a
different channel, a personal call instead of an automated message, or a
shortened gap before it) on a sample of leads, and compare stage-5 survival
before rolling out broadly.

Visualized live in the dashboard (`dashboard/app.py`).

## Work Package 6 — Budget Optimization Simulator

Full detail: [`docs/budget_optimizer_findings.md`](docs/budget_optimizer_findings.md)

Profit is **not monotonic in budget** - campaigns budgeted 2,000-5,000
average roughly 4-10x the profit of campaigns budgeted either lower or
higher (e.g. 3,000 -> ~21,980 avg. profit; 8,000 -> ~5,492). Simulating a
50,000 monthly budget split across equal-sized campaigns (bounded to stay
within the 500-20,000 historical range):

- **Most concentrated** (3 campaigns of 16,667 each): 15,257 predicted total profit
- **Most spread** (100 campaigns of 500 each): 113,042 predicted total profit
- **Optimal (25 campaigns of 2,000 each): 543,719 predicted total profit**

**Bottom line**: neither extreme wins. Both full concentration and full
spread push the per-campaign budget outside the 2,000-5,000 sweet spot; a
moderate number of medium-sized campaigns beats the better of the two
extremes by roughly 430,000 in predicted profit. Interactive version in
the dashboard (`dashboard/pages/2_Budget_Simulator.py`).

## Consolidated business recommendations

1. **Re-tier ad spend around the 2,000-5,000 sweet spot.** This is the
   highest-leverage lever found in the entire analysis (Work Packages 1
   and 6 agree independently). Avoid both very small campaigns (<1,500)
   and very large ones (>6,000).
2. **Fix the `followup_4` -> `followup_5` handoff.** It's the single
   biggest point of lead loss, and it happens right before the only gate
   that determines whether a deal can close. Pilot a change before a full
   rollout (Work Package 5).
3. **Target upsell campaigns using the ML model, not a simple rule.** The
   CatBoost/XGBoost/LightGBM classifiers roughly double the F1 of a
   "high LTV, low CAC" heuristic - the heuristic misses most real
   opportunities (Work Package 3).
4. **Use the Super-Customer score to prioritize retention/referral effort**
   on leads most likely to stay long, upsell, and refer others, via the
   live `POST /api/score-lead` endpoint (Work Package 4).
5. **Forecast revenue potential per customer with the LTV model**
   (LightGBM/CatBoost, R²=0.946) for capacity and revenue planning - never
   feed `cumulative_profit` back into any of these models; it's only known
   after the fact and would silently invalidate the forecast (Work Package 2).
